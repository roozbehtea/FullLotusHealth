Pain and tensionBack and neck pain, tight shoulders, headaches, old injuries

Pain is one of the most common reasons people try acupuncture. Treatment works on the area that hurts and on what keeps it tight, such as stress, poor sleep, or an old injury that never fully settled. Cupping, moxibustion or massage are added when they help.

Expect a short series of visits rather than a single fix. For pain that keeps coming back, herbs can carry the work between visits.

Stress, anxiety and sleepFeeling wired or worn out, trouble falling or staying asleep

Chinese medicine treats stress as something the whole body carries, not only the mind. Acupuncture helps the nervous system shift out of a stressed state, and the quiet rest during treatment is part of the work.

For sleep, an herbal formula often carries the effect between visits. We also look at digestion and daily rhythm, which affect sleep more than most people expect.

Digestion and gut healthBloating, IBS, reflux, irregular bowels

In the school of Chinese medicine I practice, digestion sits at the center. When it struggles, energy, sleep, mood and skin often follow, so for many people this is where treatment starts.

Herbal medicine usually leads, matched to your pattern and adjusted as things change, with acupuncture and practical food guidance alongside. This works well as an online consultation.

Fatigue and low energyRunning on empty, slow recovery after illness, tired after a full night's sleep

Low energy has many roots: digestion, sleep, stress, hormones, or a body that never fully recovered from an illness. The first visit is about finding which one is driving yours.

Herbs usually do the steady rebuilding, with acupuncture to support it. Travel fatigue and jet lag get a shorter plan.

Hormones and women's healthPainful or irregular cycles, PMS, perimenopause and menopause

Chinese medicine pays close attention to the menstrual cycle, which shows a lot about your overall health. Treatment follows your cycle, usually combining acupuncture with an herbal formula adjusted month to month.

For perimenopause and menopause, treatment focuses on hot flashes, sleep and mood, built around your pattern rather than a fixed protocol.

Acupuncture

Your first acupuncture visit

  1. Consultation

    We begin with your health history, lifestyle, and goals, and map out a treatment plan.

  2. Gentle placement

    Ultra-fine, sterile needles are gently placed. Most patients feel only a slight sensation or nothing at all.

  3. Restorative rest

    You rest for 20 to 30 minutes in a calm room while the treatment works.

  4. Afterward

    We finish with herbal recommendations if they suit you, and often a moment in the tea space.

Chinese herbal medicine

How herbal medicine works here

Most medicine asks what the symptom is. Chinese medicine asks why the system is out of balance. Your formula is written for your pattern, not for the name of a condition, and it changes as you do.

  1. A full intake

    In person or by video, we go through your history, digestion, sleep, energy and what makes things better or worse. Please complete the new patient intake form first.

  2. A formula for your pattern

    You receive an herbal formula matched to you, with practical food guidance where it helps.

  3. Follow up and refine

    We adjust your herbs as your body responds, aiming for steady change that lasts.
